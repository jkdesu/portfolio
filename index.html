About Me

I am a student at Columbia University GSAPP majoring in Computational Design. Throughout my coursework, I have developed a strong interest in human-centered interaction, which has expanded my research into spatial interaction design. Currently, I am researching methods to visualize volatility using Bitcoin as a proof of concept. My goal is to create immersive environments where people can experience volatility through sensory interactions and spatial equipment. Prior to GSAPP, I majored in Economics at Waseda University in Tokyo.

As part of my coursework, I developed Volatility of Everyday Life—an interactive project that combines real-time price visualization with facial-expression analytics to investigate how people emotionally respond to price differences between Tokyo and New York City. The project uses browser-based computer vision and dynamic data rendering to quantify emotional volatility as a rate of change in users' reactions.

Research

Consumerism and Scarcity

My thesis research began by exploring the complex relationship between Consumerism and Scarcity, examining how scarcity influences consumer behavior and market dynamics. This foundational research has evolved into an interactive thesis project centered on Bitcoin Volatility. I am now expanding this work through installation art and projection mapping, creating immersive spatial experiences that allow audiences to viscerally understand market volatility. This ongoing research is part of my master's degree program in Computational Design at GSAPP, Columbia University.
